Knowledge and scepticism

In F. Jackson and M. Smith (eds.), The Oxford Handbook of Contemporary Philosophy (2005), 681–700. Oxford: Oxford University Press.

Google Scholar: 60 citations As of 20 August 2026.

Scepticism is a disease in which healthy mental processes run pathologically unchecked. Our cognitive immunity system, designed to protect our conception of the world from harmful errors, turns destructively on that conception itself. Since we have false beliefs, we benefit from the ability to detect our mistakes; removing our errors tends to do us good. Our cognitive immunity system should be able to destroy bad old beliefs, not just prevent the influx of bad new ones. But that ability sometimes becomes indiscriminate, and destroys good beliefs too. That can happen in several ways. This article starts by considering two of them.

Keywords: scepticism, mental processes, immunity system, cognitive system, false beliefs, beliefs
